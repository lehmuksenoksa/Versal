Items carry tags as monsters do (round 10: the same tags for everything). Six kinds, from your
examples and today's traits. Keep, maybe or drop each tag; then three questions on how they combine.

## My critique

- **They're the variety.** Twelve kinds of weapon times a few tags of each kind make hundreds of
  different finds from little to draw: the tags change the name and the numbers, not the picture.
- **They tie into depth and place.** Crude and goblin-made are shallow; mithril, masterwork and the
  rare enchantments are deep. Dwarven things in the Keep, dark elven ones in the Deep Dark. So a
  find tells you where you are.
- **They tie into the systems.** Silver and slaying answer particular monsters; "of light" is a lamp
  without oil (the run's clock); "of silence" and "of shadows" serve the sneaking.
- **Names get long.** "A rusty fine dwarven steel dagger of spider slaying" is too much. A limit
  keeps names readable (question 2).

## Quality

- **Crude**: Worse than plain: goblin work, or ruined
- **Fine**: A little better
- **Masterwork**: The best a hand can make

## Maker

- **Goblin-made**: Crude by nature; common up top
- **Dwarven**: Sturdy and heavy; the Keep's
- **Dark elven**: Light and keen; the Deep Dark's
- **Ancient**: From the old kings' tombs: strange and old
- **Cultist**: Marked with the cult's signs; cursed more often

## Material

- **Bronze**: Old and soft
- **Iron**: Plain: what most things are (left unsaid in a name)
- **Steel**: Harder, keener
- **Silver**: Bites the dead and the eldritch
- **Mithril**: Light as cloth, hard as steel; deep

## Condition

- **Rusty**: Worse (in the game)
- **Sharp**: Makes foes bleed (in the game)
- **Heavy**: Harder blows, harder to hit with (in the game)
- **Sturdy**: Better protection (in the game)
- **Padded**: Fewer bleeds (in the game)
- **Spiked**: Hurts who hits you (in the game)
- **Cursed**: Worse, and won't come off (in the game, hidden)
- **Notched**: A little worse, cheap to mend

## Slaying ("of … slaying")

- **Of spider slaying**: Bites harder on spiders
- **Of goblin slaying**: On goblinkin (goblin-bane, in the game)
- **Of undead slaying**: On the dead
- **Of beast slaying**: On beasts
- **Of dragon slaying**: On the dragon: rare, deep
- **Of elf slaying**: On the dark elves
- **Of horror slaying**: On the eldritch

## Enchantment ("of …")

- **Of light**: Glows: a light you carry, no oil
- **Of fire**: Burns what it hits
- **Of silence**: Its blows make no noise
- **Of shadows**: On armour: you're harder to see
- **Of swiftness**: A little faster
- **Of venom**: Poisons what it hits
- **Of returning**: Thrown, it comes back
- **Of warding**: The dead keep back

## Questions

1. **The six kinds:** each kind's tags, as above.
2. **How many tags on one item:**
   - **A** up to two words before its name and one "of …" after: "a dwarven masterwork dagger of
     fire" *(my pick)*;
   - **B** one word before and one "of …";
   - **C** any number.
3. **Do tags follow depth?** Crude and goblin-made shallow; mithril, masterwork and the rare
   enchantments deep; makers by region. *My pick: yes*, through the same danger ladder as monsters.
4. **The order of words:** condition, quality, maker, material, the thing, then "of …": "a rusty
   dwarven axe", "a fine steel sword of fire". *My pick: yes.* Plain iron and plain quality are left
   unsaid.
