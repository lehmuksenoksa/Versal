# The places, organised

All the places kept so far (rounds 4 and 5), organised three ways:
- **where they fit** (by region);
- **what they allow in play**;
- **what they tell.**

This is my proposal of fits, to be corrected. Nothing here is a decision about the details of a
place (what's in it, who, how it's drawn); each of those is a later question.

## Principles (yours, round 5)

- **The map is generated**: every run different.
- **Regions with a different feel, and a bit different play.**
- **Surprises on each run, good ones as well as bad.**

## Your ideas from round 5's comments

- **A pickaxe:** "I'd be great if the player could find a pick axe and mine new tunnels."
- **Friendly characters and trade:** "A market could be nice, or a shop. We need ways to introduce
  friendly characters as well. Goblins should be enemies though."
- **The map room fills in your map:** "The map could fill in the player's remembered map."
- **The great stairway:** "This could a part of a great dwarven keep."
- **The feast hall:** kept "if we get the looks right".

Each of these is on round 6's lists, as a question of its own.

## Overlaps to settle later

Some regions are also smaller places; a later question is whether they are regions or areas:
- the pits (beast pens, the fighting pit);
- the forge (the smithy);
- the sewers (the cistern);
- the webs (a spider nest).

## Thin regions

By the table below, some regions have few places of their own:
- the forge;
- the sulphur vents;
- the rift;
- the pits;
- the webs.

The dwarven hold, the dungeon and the bandits' hold have many. A thin region either needs places
of its own (a later brainstorm), or is better as an area inside another region. That is the same
later question as the overlaps above.
