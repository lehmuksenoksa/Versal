# Round 13: a world by depth, the regions set in it

Your idea: "If we widen the scope, the regions could be set in a more generic, random generated
world that is based on depth. Then the regions would stand out."

So there are two layers:
- **The world:** generic, generated by depth: what's under the ground at that depth.
- **The regions:** set in it, standing out against it. So are the transitions and themed areas.

## My critique

- **The generic world is mostly built already.** Today a level already changes its ground by
  depth (built rooms, then collapsed, then caves). The generic world is that generator, carried
  deeper.
- **Danger follows depth again.** Round 12 lost "deeper is harder" when maps linked sideways. In
  the generic world it comes back on its own, and a region can be set to the depths that suit it.
- **Regions stand out by contrast.** That's the point of your idea: they get the hand-made feel,
  the world gives the plain ground between.
- **The generic world can feel like filler.** This is the risk. It still needs what round 6
  kept:
  - surprises;
  - strangers;
  - themed areas;
  - its monsters by depth.

  It's plain by comparison, not empty.
- **"Widen the scope":** more maps means either a longer run or smaller maps. Round 1 set 1 to 2
  hours; question 5 asks whether that holds.
- **For the code:** two kinds of generator.
  - A depth-driven one for the world: today's, extended.
  - One per region: new.

  The world map is a column of depth with regions joined to it, which the save stores as the list
  of maps and their links.

## Questions

Each is separate.

1. **A generic world by depth, with the regions set in it?** *My pick: yes.*
2. **How a region sits in the world:**
   - **A** it replaces the world's maps at its depth: you pass through it;
   - **B** it branches off the world: entered by a side way or a stair, and left the same way;
   - **C** both, region by region *(my pick)*.

   **Why C:** some regions are a way down (the Dungeon Underneath), others a place you go into
   and come back from (the Drowned City). With branching (round 12), the side ways are where the
   route choices are.
3. **How much of a run is the generic world:**
   - **A** most, with regions as high points;
   - **B** about half *(my pick)*;
   - **C** little, the world only joins the regions.

   **Why B:** enough world for the regions to stand out against, not so much that it drags.
4. **What the world is made of by depth:**
   - **A** today's kinds of ground, carried deeper (built, collapsed, caves, and something deeper
     still) *(my pick)*;
   - **B** something else (say in the comment).
5. **The run's length:**
   - **A** stays 1 to 2 hours (round 1), so the maps are smaller or fewer *(my pick)*;
   - **B** grows to 2 to 4 hours.

   **Why A:** a run that can be finished in an evening suits a roguelike, and saving (round 1)
   covers stopping halfway.
