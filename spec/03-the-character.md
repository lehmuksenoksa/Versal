# 3. The character

One start for everyone. The run's variety comes from choices made at each level up (round 1).

## Decided (round 2)

- **Levels are earned by going deeper and finding things out.** Experience for:
  - new ground seen, and each new level reached;
  - secrets found and notes read;
  - sneaking: getting past a monster that would have noticed you, and sneak attacks;
  - bosses, killed or got past.

  Kills give a little.
- **At a level up, one of three offered:** three choices drawn from a pool (about 30 to 40),
  grouped in families so the player can lean into one. Rare or strong ones come only deeper. A
  page shows the three.
- **Attributes, with the numbers worked out from them.** In your words: "Health, attack, defense,
  speed, sneakiness, mana/magic derived from attributes. I'd like them to be a bit different (more
  flavorful) than traditionally:"
  - **Grit**
  - **Brawn**
  - **Cunning**
  - **Fervor**
  - **Acumen**

---

## 3.4 What each attribute drives (round 3)

My proposal, so that each attribute matters to a different way of playing and none does
everything:

| Attribute | What it is | Drives |
|---|---|---|
| **Grit** | Toughness, holding on | **Health.** Resisting bleeding and poison. Blows taken (part of **defence**, with armour) |
| **Brawn** | Strength | **Damage** of blows. Heavy weapons and armour without their penalties. Shoving. How much you carry |
| **Cunning** | Quickness, guile | **Sneakiness** (how hard you are to notice). **Hitting** (with any weapon, thrown or shot). Traps and locks |
| **Fervor** | Will, faith, zeal | **Mana** (half). Resisting dread (2.4 B) and fear. Standing against the dead and the cult (curses, their magic) |
| **Acumen** | Mind, learning | **Mana** (half). The power of scrolls, wands and spells. Knowing things: unknown things found out sooner, more told in the tips. Noticing secrets and traps |

**Speed** is not worked out from an attribute. In a turn-based game, small changes to speed change
everything (an extra move every few turns decides most fights and chases). So speed is moved only
by particular things: heavy armour, swiftness, a level-up choice.

Critique:
- **Cunning is the strongest pull in a stealth game.** Sneakiness and hitting both come from it.
  Kept in check by:
  - damage coming from Brawn;
  - health from Grit;
  - noticing traps from Acumen.

  Simulated runs should check that an all-Cunning character isn't simply best.
- **Five attributes and six numbers to show.** The character sheet in the ledger shows both. The
  tips explain where a number comes from.
- **Every rule says what it reads.** That's the cost of attributes over plain numbers (round 2's
  critique). Content tables (part 5) list the attribute for each thing.

Options:
- **A**, as in the table *(my pick)*.
- **B**, as in the table, but speed from Cunning.
- Or change any line in the comment.

## 3.5 How attributes grow (round 3)

| | Option | Costs and consequences |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Each level up: one of three choices, and +1 to an attribute of your own** *(my pick)* | Both at once: the attribute is steady growth you steer, and the choice is the run's colour. One page for both. |
| B | **The three offered mix attributes and other choices** | A level up is either growth or a new ability, never both. Simpler, but a +1 offer is usually the dull pick. |
| C | **Attributes rise only by things** (charms, potions, choices that raise them) | Attributes are set at the start and move little. Finds matter more, and growth is uneven between runs. |

Where they start: everyone starts the same (round 1). My proposal is all five at the same middle
value. The first few level ups are where a character becomes yours.

## 3.6 Magic (round 3)

Mana (decided) needs something to spend it on. Spells for a stealth game:
- **darkness** (a patch of the dark where you need it);
- **silence** (your steps make no noise for a while);
- **douse** (put out a light from afar);
- **sleep**;
- **blink** (a step of a few squares);
- **sight through walls**;
- **a ward against the dead**.

| | Option | Costs and consequences |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Things only:** scrolls and wands are the magic; mana isn't spent | Least work, but then mana is a number that does nothing, and should go. |
| B | **Spells, one mana pool** (from Fervor and Acumen) *(my pick)* | Spells learned from level-up choices and scrolls (reading one teaches it or casts it once: part 5). Mana comes back slowly, faster at rest. A list of known spells in the ledger. Each spell drawn where it shows on the map (part 8). **L** |
| C | **Two kinds:** prayers (Fervor: against the dead, the cult, dread) and the arcane (Acumen: darkness, silence, blink), each with its own pool | The two attributes stand apart, and the cult's story gets a magic of its own to face. Twice the lists and balance; two gauges. **L** on top. |

**Why B:** it gives both Fervor and Acumen a reason to raise them, with one pool to show and
balance. C can grow out of B later by sorting the spells into two kinds.
