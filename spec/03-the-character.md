# 3. The character (proposed, round 2)

One start for everyone. The run's variety comes from choices made at each level up (round 1).
Asked on the choices page, round 2.

---

## 3.1 How you earn a level

Today, experience comes from kills. In a stealth game that pays the player to fight, against the
heart of the game.

| | Option | Costs and consequences |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Kills** (as now) | Simple. But every monster sneaked past is experience lost, so a careful player is weaker. |
| B | **Going deeper and finding things out** *(my pick)* | Experience for: new ground seen, each new level reached, secrets found, notes read, and also for sneaking (getting past a monster that would have noticed you, a sneak attack) and bosses (killed or got past). Kills give a little. It rewards the way the game is meant to be played. Needs a tally of each, and its balance (simulated runs). **S** |
| C | **A level up for each level reached** | Fixed: about 10 level ups a run, one per level. The simplest to balance, but nothing you do changes how fast you grow. |

## 3.2 What you choose at a level up

| | Option | Costs and consequences |
|---|---|---|
| A | **One of three offered:** three choices drawn from a pool, you take one *(my pick)* | Every run grows differently. The pool needs about 30 to 40 choices, each designed and balanced. A page showing three. Rare or strong choices can come only deeper. **L** |
| B | **A tree:** a few paths (for example shadow, blade, lamp, lore), each a line of choices that open in order; you pick where to go next | Builds planned from the start, the same each time. Less to design than A (about 20), but runs grow alike once you know the tree. A page showing the tree. **M** |
| C | **Points into stats** | Numbers only: health, attack, defence, stealth. Least work, and least interest. **S** |

**Why A:** with one start for everyone, the choices are what makes one run unlike another, so they
shouldn't come in a fixed order. The pool can still be grouped in families (shadow, blade, lamp,
lore, endurance), so the player can lean into one.

What a choice is, for example (to be written in part 5):
- *Soft step*: sneaking costs no extra time.
- *Night eyes*: you see one more square in the dark.
- *Backstab*: a sneak attack does four times the damage instead of three.
- *Steady hand*: the lamp burns half as much oil.
- *Thick skin*: +1 defence.
- *Lore of the dead*: the undead notice you less.

## 3.3 What a character has

| | Option | Costs and consequences |
|---|---|---|
| A | **A few numbers, the choices changing rules** *(my pick)*: health, attack, defence, speed (as now), and what you've chosen changes how things work (as the examples above) | Few numbers to show and balance. The choices carry the interest. |
| B | **Attributes** (strength, agility, wits and so on) that the choices raise, and everything worked out from them | The familiar way. More numbers to show, and each rule has to say which attribute it reads. **M** |
| C | **Skills with ranks** (stealth, blades, lore...), each rising with choices | Clear growth in each skill, but stealth becomes a number to grind rather than a way of playing. |
