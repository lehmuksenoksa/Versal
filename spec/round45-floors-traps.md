# Round 45: slice 1's looks: floors and traps

The first of slice 1's looks (`slice1-looks.md`), families first (part 0): how a floor tells its sound,
deep water and a current, and how a trap shows it's there to act on (your rule, round 43). Drawn with
the game's own pen in `mockups/floors.html`; the pictures are on the choices page
(`mockups/choices/fl_*.jpg`, `wa_*.jpg`, `tr_*.jpg`, `trs_*.jpg`).

## Floors: how a floor tells its sound

Rubble, bones, wooden boards, moss, shallow water: the loud ones and the quiet one (round 2: floors drawn so their sound can be read). Drawn with the game's own pen.

- **A**: Each floor its own marks, all alike in weight
- **B**: Loud floors heavier and denser; the quiet one light (my pick)
- **C**: As A, and the quiet floor a faint green wash

**Why B:** the sound is what matters in play, so it should read at a glance: heavy, dense marks for loud floors, light ones for quiet. Each floor keeps its own marks, so you also know what it is. **A** tells what a floor is, but not how loud. **C** uses colour, which the visual language keeps for meaning (green for quiet would be a new meaning to learn).

## Deep water

Shallow (left), deep (middle), deep with a current (right). Deep water puts your light out when you swim (features.md), so it must be told from shallow at once. This question is about the deep water only.

- **A**: A darker wash
- **B**: A darker wash, an inked edge, and drops (my pick)

**Why B:** a darker wash alone is easy to miss in dim light; an inked edge and the drops make deep water its own thing.

## A current

The right-hand pool in each: a current moves you or a monster along it. This question is about how its way is shown.

- **Streaks**: Long streaks along the flow (my pick)
- **Arrowheads**: Streaks with arrowheads

**Why streaks:** long streaks read as flow and keep to the pen; arrowheads tell the way at once but are a sign, not a drawing (the visual language keeps letters and signs few).

## Traps: how a trap shows it's there to act on

A tripwire (with its bell), a pressure plate, a net folded above, as seen. Your rule from round 43 applies: things to act on look different from decoration.

- **A**: Ink, like any feature
- **B**: In red, the danger colour
- **C**: Ink, with a ring of ticks: a thing to act on (my pick)

**Why C:** the ring of ticks can be the one mark for everything you can act on (traps, containers, a lever), so the rule is one sign learned once. **B**'s red says danger, but only traps can use it. **A** doesn't follow your rule.

## Traps, sprung

The same three spent: the wire snapped, the plate sunk with darts about, the net cast.

- **As drawn**: As drawn, in ink, the mark gone (my pick)
- **In red**: Kept in red (goes with B above)

**Why as drawn:** a spent trap stays a thing you can read (the net is still there to cut through) and loses its mark, since there's nothing left to act on.
