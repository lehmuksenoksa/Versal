# 2. The run (proposed, round 2)

What a run goes through, top to bottom: the reason to go down, the regions and their bosses, and
what keeps a run moving. Asked on the choices page, round 2.

Built on round 1:
- stealth in the dark;
- about 10 levels in 3 or 4 regions, a boss closing each;
- a thin story thread;
- one saved run.

Work is weighed **S**, **M**, **L**, **XL**.

---

## 2.1 The reason to go down, and the bottom

The thread the story hangs on. It decides the last region.

| | Option | What it means | Costs and consequences |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **The cult** *(my pick)* | The cultists, already on the first level with their lanterns, serve something sleeping under the goblin king's halls. They raise the dead in the crypts, buy the goblins' help, and are waking it. You go down to stop them. The last region is their sanctum. The end: the waking thing, or its high priest. | Uses what's there: the cultists become the thread through every region (their notes, their marks on walls, their patrols). One new region to design and draw (the sanctum: carved, with pools and ritual circles), **L**. |
| B | **Something to fetch and bring back up** | A relic at the bottom. Taking it wakes the levels above, and you have to climb back out through them. | The climb back doubles the use of each level (cheaper content), but levels already explored are less tense. Every level has to be kept and changed (monsters returned, new ones), which means more saving and memory. The way up needs its own rules, **L**. |
| C | **The goblin king is the end** | As now: the king's halls are the bottom. The regions above lead there. | No new region at the bottom, but 10 levels over 3 regions means each region is 3 or 4 levels of the same kind of ground. The end is a goblin, which is weak as a final threat. |

**Why A:** it turns something already in the game into the thread, and gives a final region that
can be about light and dark (a sanctum lit by the cult's fires, or deep dark) at the end of a
stealth game.

## 2.2 The regions

With A above, I'd propose four regions, about ten levels:

| Region | Levels | Ground (from today's) | Who lives there | Closed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The dungeon | 1–3 | Built rooms, turning to collapsed | The dead (skeletons, zombies), rats, the first cultists | The warden of the crypts (the dead's master) |
| 2. The caverns | 4–5 | Collapsed, turning to caverns | Spiders, bats, rats, the first goblins | The brood mother (a great spider) |
| 3. The king's halls | 6–8 | Caverns, turning to the halls | Goblins, warriors, trolls | The goblin king |
| 4. The sanctum | 9–10 | **New:** the cult's carved temple | Cultists, the dead they raise, what they serve | The waking thing, or its high priest |

The bosses and monsters are a first sketch; they're settled in part 5. What's asked here is the
shape: four regions, the order, three of today's kinds of ground and one new one.

Options:
- **A**, as in the table *(my pick)*.
- **B**, three regions (no caverns region, the caves only between the dungeon and the halls).
  Less new work, but less change over the run.
- **C**, five regions, adding a second new one (for example a drowned region, flooded ruins).
  More to draw (**L**).

## 2.3 What a boss is in a stealth game

| | Option | Costs and consequences |
|---|---|---|
| A | **A boss guards the way down:** it can be killed or got past (sneaked by, lured away). Killing it gives more (its hoard, a level up) *(my pick)* | Each boss's lair laid out with routes past it (part 6). A boss that can be got round keeps to the heart of the game. |
| B | **A boss must be killed** to go on | Simpler levels. But a sneaking character who has avoided fights faces a forced fight, which goes against the heart. Balance has to make every character able to win it. |
| C | **Bosses optional,** off the main way, with a prize | The least pressure. The regions lose their ending. |

## 2.4 What keeps a run moving

Without pressure, a careful player rests to full after each fight and can wait out anything. That
takes the tension out of stealth. More than one can be picked.

| | Option | Costs and consequences |
|---|---|---|
| A | **The lamp's oil** *(my pick)* | The lamp burns oil while lit; oil is found on the levels. Out of oil, you're in the dark for good (sneaking, seeing little) until you find more. Light becomes the run's resource, which is the heart of the game. Needs: an oil gauge in the ledger, oil as an item, and the balance of how much oil there is (simulated runs). **M** |
| B | **The level wakes up over time** *(my pick, with A)* | The longer you stay on a level, the more its monsters wander, wake and patrol; resting is a risk, not a pause. Needs a level-wide "alert" that rises with time and noise, which the monsters' minds (part 4) read. **M** |
| C | **Hunger** | Food to find and eat. The classic clock, but it adds an item and a gauge that has nothing to do with light or sneaking. **M** |
| D | **None** | As now. |

**Why A and B:** the oil makes light, the game's heart, a thing you spend. The waking level
makes waiting dangerous, without a separate clock. Resting in the dark saves oil but lets the
level wake: the two pull against each other, which is the kind of choice a stealth game wants.
