"I feel like we need more thematic coherence here, between enemies, strangers and rooms/areas.
We have the dead: crypts, skeletons, zombies, ossuaries etc. We have the religious: temples,
altars, friendly priest, cultists, pilgrims, holy warriors. We have pests: spiders, rats,
ratcatcher. We have the castle like dungeon: guard rooms, barracks, prison (and jail), halls,
kitchen, larder. Is this overtaken by cultists or guarded by undead left there to rot or
something else?"

So: **themes**. A theme bundles foes, strangers and places that belong together. A region is a
mix of a few themes. A themed area is one theme, concentrated.

## My critique

- **It's what was missing.** Regions said where; themes say what goes with what. A crypt comes
  with its dead, and with the holy warriors who come to put them to rest.
- **It fits the tags.** A theme can be one more tag on monsters, strangers and places (round 10's
  "the same tags for everything"). Nothing new to build.
- **It's how the generator should work.** It picks a region's themes, then draws the rooms and
  the ones who live in them from the same themes. So what you meet makes sense where you meet it,
  run after run.
- **It answers "who guards it?"** Your round 14 comments asked it of the guard post and the
  prison, and "no cooks" in the kitchen. A place's occupants come from its theme.
- **Some things belong to two themes.** A prisoner belongs to whoever holds the prison. Themes are
  tags, so a thing can carry more than one.

## Questions

Each is separate.

1. **Themes as the way content is organised** (a theme bundles foes, strangers and places; regions
   mix themes; a themed area is one theme)? *My pick: yes.*
2. **The religious: one theme or two?**
   - **A** one, the religious;
   - **B** two, **the old faith** (temples, the friendly priest, pilgrims, holy warriors) and
     **the cult** (cultists, the sanctum, sacrifice), set against each other *(my pick)*.

   **Why B:** an old faith and a cult that has turned its temples is a story the map can tell
   without words. Holy warriors hunt cultists and their raised dead.
3. **Who holds the castle-like dungeon:**
   - **A** the dead garrison: the guards died at their posts and rose, still walking their old
     rounds, the prisoners still in the cells;
   - **B** the cult: they took it as their stronghold, with the prison for their captives and the
     raised dead as their guards;
   - **C** outlaws: bandits use it as their hideout;
   - **D** goblins, come up from below;
   - **E** one of them drawn each run *(my pick)*.

   **Why E:** the same rooms, held by someone different each run, is a surprise the rules can
   make (your principles, round 5). Each holder guards differently: the dead keep old rounds, the
   cult keeps captives, the outlaws keep watch for their own. The rooms are drawn once; the
   holders vary. If E, which of A to D can be drawn is asked in the next question.
4. **Which holders can be drawn** (if E): pick any of A to D. *My pick: A, B and C.* Goblins
   are for Goblin Territory.
5. **The themes**, each to keep, maybe or drop.
